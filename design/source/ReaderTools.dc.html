Walden Where I Lived, and What I Lived For · part 14 of 62
Aa

Previously Thoreau nearly bought the Hollowell farm, then chose the woods instead — moving in on the Fourth of July, 1845.

I WENT TO THE WOODS because I wished to live deliberately, to front only the essential facts of life, and see if I could not learn what it had to teach, and not, when I came to die, discover that I had not lived. I did not wish to live what was not life, living is so dear; nor did I wish to practise resignation, unless it was quite necessary.

Front is used here as a verb — to face something squarely. Thoreau isn’t studying life from a distance; he wants to meet it head-on and see what’s left once everything inessential is stripped away.

Written during his two years at Walden Pond, 1845–47. Published 1854.

I wanted to live deep and suck out all the marrow of life, to live so sturdily and Spartan-like as to put to rout all that was not life, to cut a broad swath and shave close, to drive life into a corner, and reduce it to its lowest terms, and, if it proved to be mean, why then to get the whole and genuine meanness of it, and publish its meanness to the world; or if it were sublime, to know it by experience, and be able to give a true account of it in my next excursion.
